## The four content missions

Every post serves ONE primary mission — the job it does for the business. The post's **primary purpose** decides its mission — not its topic, format or call to action. The same project or problem can serve any of the four missions, depending on what the post is for.

### attract — reach new audiences
Purpose: help relevant people who don't know the studio discover it, through content that catches attention, feels relatable or sparks curiosity.
Typical angles: relatable problems, struggles or challenges; actions, attempts or decisions taken to address them; outcomes, transformations or learnings; problem → pursuit → payoff stories; trending formats adapted to a real story.
Example: "Why do small bathrooms often feel even smaller?" — common design challenges and interesting solutions people recognise and share.
Key distinction: the problem creates interest for new people. The post doesn't need to promote the service or ask for an inquiry.

### nurture — build connection and trust
Purpose: help people get to know the studio, understand its values and trust it over time.
Typical angles: behind-the-scenes work; ongoing challenges and difficulties; ambitions and goals; effort and progress; trade-offs and everyday realities; what's happening now and why it matters; values guiding project decisions; the people and the studio's day.
Example: "What happened behind the scenes during our latest renovation?" — real challenges, decisions and progress.
Key distinction: the goal is familiarity, connection and trust — not grabbing strangers' attention, and not demonstrating expertise.

### position — demonstrate expertise
Purpose: help the audience recognise the studio's professional knowledge, judgment and distinctive approach.
- Expert POV (established knowledge, experience or demonstrated skill): insights from professional experience, proven practices or results, actionable advice, design decisions and their reasoning, constraints, trade-offs and processes, material comparisons.
- Journey POV (genuine learning, not yet established expertise): what the studio is learning, experiments and tests, mistakes and improvements, lessons from real projects.
Example: "Why we chose a sliding door instead of a traditional door for this bathroom." — the constraints, the alternatives and the reasoning.
Key distinction: the post shows how the studio thinks and decides, so potential clients understand the value of its expertise. Never invent expertise, experience or results.

### convert — encourage business inquiries
Purpose: help interested potential clients see how the studio can address THEIR needs, and encourage a relevant commercial next step.
Possible elements (use what fits, not a required structure): a specific problem potential clients face; its consequences; the outcome clients want; how the studio's actual service or method addresses it; how that works, backed by verified evidence; a clear next action (discuss a project, request a consultation).
Example: "Planning to renovate a small bathroom? Here's how our studio approaches limited spaces." — the studio's actual approach, its relevant capability, an invitation to discuss the project.
Key distinction: convert connects a potential client's problem to the studio's actual service. The service offer is the subject of the post, not a sign-off.

### The same project, four missions
| Mission | Content angle | Intended outcome |
|---|---|---|
| attract | Why small bathrooms feel smaller than they are | Reach relevant new people |
| nurture | The challenges we faced during this renovation | Build connection and trust |
| position | Why we chose a sliding door to improve the layout | Demonstrate design judgment |
| convert | How our studio helps clients plan functional small bathrooms | Encourage project inquiries |

### Mission rules
1. **One primary mission per post.** A post can have secondary benefits, but it has one clear strategic purpose.
2. **A CTA does not define the mission.** "Contact us", "DM us", "link in bio", "book a consultation" added to an attract, nurture or position post does NOT make it convert. Judge what the body of the post does. A post is convert only when its main content is about the studio's service for a client's need.
3. **Do not force mission coverage.** An under-represented mission is recommended only when a genuine opportunity exists.
4. **Respect the capture.** Choose angles supported by real information, expertise, experiences and assets.
5. **Avoid unnecessary repetition.** Different missions can use the same project when they say genuinely distinct things.
6. **Success depends on the mission.** Reach, engagement, demonstrated interest in expertise and business inquiries are different outcomes.

Core principle: recommend content based on what the studio needs to achieve and what it can genuinely communicate — not simply to complete a mission distribution.
