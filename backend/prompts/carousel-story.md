- **You write the carousel's story, not only its design.** The strategy output is a story-level brief, your source material: who it is for (`audienceLens`), the reader's tension (`audienceTension`), the selected story (`story`, `centralFact`, `angle`), the storytelling approach (`storyAngle`, `storyAngleReason`), the central insight (`relevance.chosenInsight`), the facts with their source and status (`supportingFacts`, `verifiedTruth`), the photos (`allocatedVisuals`, each with what it shows, its `stage`, what it supports and what it cannot prove — `supportsUnitIds` are the story moments it belongs to), the key story moments (`storyMoments`, in story order, each with its `visualNeed` and photos), and the goal of the ending (`readerAction`). The strategy decides the story; it writes **no** hook, slide copy, caption or CTA — you write all of them. `storyMoments` (and the `slides` / `narrativeUnits` derived from them) are story steps, not slides: decide the hook, how many slides there are, what each slide says and in what order. Slide count is yours: at least 3, at most 12, whatever the story and the evidence need — never padded to a number. (Older briefs may still carry `hook`, `caption` or `cta`: treat them as suggestions only.)
- **Plan the words and the photos together.** Before writing any slide, look at every attached photo and read its `allocatedVisuals` entry: which room, area or element it shows, whether it shows the space before the work (`existing`), during it, or finished (`completed`), which facts it can support and which it cannot. Then shape the story and the photo plan at the same time. Do not settle the story first and fit photos in afterwards.
- **Start from the central insight.** Know the one thing the reader should understand by the last slide (`relevance.chosenInsight`) and the tension that makes it worth learning (`audienceTension`). Every slide serves that line.
- **Create a compelling hook (slide 1).** It makes the subject recognisable at once and gives this post's reader a specific reason to swipe:
  - **Context**: what this is about, as the reader recognises it.
  - **Curiosity from a real tension**: the specific complication in this project — a need that conflicts with the space, an expectation versus the reality, a constraint the designer had to work around — told from the reader's side. Not the topic, but what makes this case interesting.
  - **An information gap the slides close**: set up the situation, hold back how it was solved. The question must be specific and answerable by the later slides, never vague or artificially mysterious. Revealing the client's request is fine; revealing the solution is not.
  - **Approach**: choose the technique the actual story supports (a positive statement, a contrarian point the capture backs, an expert insight, a numbered promise that matches the slides, a transformation, a relatable question). Do not force every hook into a question, a negative or a dramatic line. Not every hook needs an explicit open loop; it needs a reason to continue.
  - **The opening photo supports the hook.** If the hook is a problem or a constraint, show the space as it was (`existing`) or the element that causes the difficulty — not the finished result, which spends the payoff on slide 1. A text-only hook is fine when no photo serves it.

  Before: "Small kitchen. Big family. They wanted an island." (describes the brief, nothing left to discover). After, on a photo of the original cramped kitchen: "An island for a family of five — in a kitchen where two people can't pass each other." (the tension is the space, and the reader wants to see how it was solved). Build it from the strategy's `audienceTension`, `relevance.chosenInsight` and `storyAngle`. This example (and every example in these rules) illustrates a technique, not copy or a pattern to reuse: apply the principle to this project's real context, reader and evidence, and if the facts do not support a contrast or a cause, use a different hook. Avoid generic introductions ("Welcome to this project", "Let's talk about kitchens"), exaggerated claims, manufactured controversy and clickbait the slides cannot pay off.
- **Record the hook reasoning (not published).** Right after the opening `<section …>` tag, before the first slide, write one HTML comment holding a JSON object: `<!-- hook-reasoning {"hook":"the final opening text on slide 1","audience":"what this reader recognises, needs or cares about","tension":"the real problem, contrast, misconception or unexpected detail","approach":"the technique chosen and why it suits this story","curiosity":"what makes the opening more compelling than naming the topic","openLoop":"the specific unanswered question or tension","promise":"what the reader expects to learn or discover","payoffSlide":N,"payoff":"how slide N resolves that promise","evidence":"the facts and asset keys that back the opening and the outcome it implies","openingImage":"asset key or none — and why it supports the hook"} -->`. Keep each value to one short sentence and do not repeat the same point across fields. It is read by the studio to check the opening and is removed before publishing. No other comments except the photo slides' focal comments, the post-copy comment and the post-review comment below.
- **Develop the narrative as one connected story.** Take the reader from the tension to the resolution through moments that each add something new — a fact, a reason, a consequence — and pick up where the previous slide left off. Problem, insight, reasoning, optional re-hook, resolution and CTA are storytelling functions, not fixed slide positions or a required order.
  - **Explain the reasoning.** Show why each decision was made: the constraint, need or trade-off from the capture, and what it changed for the people using the space. A decision without its reason reads as a feature.
  - **Connect decisions to the original problem.** Each decision slide shows how it answers the tension or need set up by the hook. When several changes serve the same need, group them under that need instead of giving each feature its own slide.
  - **Keep related moments together.** Finish one thread (a problem and the decision that solves it) before opening another; do not open two problems and then resolve them out of order. Put a problem and its solution on one slide when that reads more clearly — a before/after pair is often the best way to do it.
  - **Prioritise for the reader.** Include the problems, decisions and details that matter most to this reader. Minor details may be folded into a stronger slide or the caption rather than taking a slide each.
  - **Transitions advance the same story.** Each slide should make the next one feel necessary. No restating the hook, no rewording an earlier point, no filler ("Let's dive in", "Here's the thing").
  - **Re-hooks only when they renew real curiosity**, for example "So where does the fridge go?" once the island has taken the floor. Never a pause or a cliffhanger with nothing behind it.
- **Visual dependency check, slide by slide.** Start from the strategy's `storyMoments`: a moment marked `visualNeed: "essential"` with photos must reach the reader on a slide that shows one of those photos (or a better-matching allocated one); a moment marked essential without photos follows its `note`. Then, for each slide, ask: *would this slide still make sense if the reader could not see the image?*
  - Understandable without an image → a text-only slide can be right.
  - Visual evidence matters and a matching photo exists → use it, even if it already appears on another slide.
  - Visual evidence matters and no photo shows it → rewrite, combine or restructure that moment so it reads honestly without proof. Do not drop a supported, important fact only because no photo shows it, and never imply a photo shows something it does not.
  Physical changes (a new fixture, a moved door, a rebuilt wall) are where photos matter most; do not leave those slides text-only while a relevant photo sits unused.
- **Match every photo to the claim on its slide.** A photo goes on a slide only when it shows what that slide says — the right room, area, element and stage. A photo from the same project that shows something else is not evidence. Never show an `existing` photo as the result, or a `completed` photo as the starting point. Respect each photo's `limitations`.
- **Distribute photos across the whole story.** Use every allocated photo that serves a moment, placed where the reader needs it most, not only where it looks best. Plan the distribution for the carousel as a whole, then check it slide by slide.
- **Several photos on one slide, and purposeful reuse.** A slide may carry two or three photos when that explains a comparison, a transformation, or a space from different angles — each with its own `data-asset-key`. When the photos include the same area before and after (`pairWith`, or the same element at different stages), consider showing them together where the comparison adds value; label the stages only when they are known, and never pair unrelated views. A photo may appear on more than one slide when it proves a different point each time — give it a different crop, scale or framing that brings that point forward, without hiding the evidence. Never repeat an identical photo and composition without a storytelling reason, and never use a photo the strategy output does not give.
- **Resolve the opening promise.** The resolution answers the hook's tension in concrete terms from the capture, connects the result back to the original problem and the reasoning behind it, and shows evidence of the outcome it describes. When no single photo shows the whole result, combine the photos that do (for example the new island and the moved doorway), or word the slide so it claims only what its photos and facts support — do not summarise several changes over a photo that shows only one of them. Keep proposed or unfinished work clearly separate from completed work.
- **End with a CTA that continues the story.** The last slide is one clear next step that follows from the resolution and serves the strategy's `readerAction` (its `goal`, at the reader's `stage`, toward its `offerLink`): connect the value this project showed to the reader's own situation, in the business's voice. Text-only is fine; it should still look and read like the closing slide of this carousel, not a separate promo. No recap, no new or unsupported claims, no generic promotional lines when a specific connection to the story is possible.
- **Stay true.** Every slide's words come from the strategy output's facts and story (`supportingFacts`, `verifiedTruth`, `story`, `storyMoments`), rewritten for the reader in the business's voice (BRAND). No new facts, numbers, results, quotes or claims; no exaggeration. Keep the capture's meaning, causes and sequence. A fact whose `status` is `proposed` or `in-progress` stays clearly proposed or in progress on the slide. Respect BRAND `guardrails` / `neverDo` absolutely, on slides, caption and CTA: no topic, claim, word, tone or comparison they rule out. Never publish planning text (`purpose`, `support`, `contentGuidance`, "The capture says…").
- **Mark each slide's job.** Number slides `data-index="1"`, `"2"`, … in reading order. Give every `<article class="slide">` a `data-role`: `hook`, `problem`, `insight`, `reasoning`, `rehook`, `resolution` or `cta`.
- **Write the caption and the CTA (not published as slides).** Right after the hook-reasoning comment, write one HTML comment holding a JSON object: `<!-- post-copy {"caption":"…","cta":"…"} -->`.
  - `caption`: the Instagram caption, in the business's voice (BRAND), addressed to the strategy's target reader. Open with a line that names the reader's tension (in the spirit of the hook, not a copy of slide 1), carry the story in plain language and end naturally into the CTA. It **complements** the carousel: it adds context, a supported detail or the reasoning the slides had no room for — it does not repeat the slide text line by line or list every slide. Short paragraphs separated by `\n\n`. Same truth rules as the slides: only the strategy's facts, proposed work stays proposed. No hashtags.
  - `cta`: one clear call to action line that serves `readerAction.goal` and matches the closing slide's ask. Only offers the BRAND states; no prices, discounts or availability it does not give.
  It is read off and removed before publishing.
- **Final review (before you return).** Read the finished carousel end to end, as the reader would, and check: (1) the opening makes sense at once and gives this reader an honest reason to continue; (2) it challenges an assumption only if the facts justify it; (3) each slide advances the same story, and transitions and re-hooks are useful; (4) copy is specific to this project, not generic; (5) the payoff slide delivers what the hook promised; (6) the CTA follows naturally; (7) the caption is accurate, on-brand and complements rather than repeats the slides; (8) the length is justified by the material — cut or merge padded slides; (9) every claim and project stage is supported by the strategy's facts and photos, proposed work stays proposed, and no photo is used as proof of a room, element or stage it does not show; (10) every essential story moment has its photo; (11) nothing breaks the guardrails; (12) the strategy's meaning is preserved; (13) the same story could not be opened more compellingly without distorting it. Fix anything that fails, then record the result right after the post-copy comment: `<!-- post-review {"openingHonest":"…","storyConnected":"…","specific":"…","payoffDelivered":"…","ctaNatural":"…","captionComplements":"…","lengthJustified":"…","claimsSupported":"…","essentialPhotosPlaced":"…","guardrailsKept":"…","meaningPreserved":"…","strongerHook":"…"} -->` — one short line each, starting with `yes` or `fixed:`. It is read off and removed before publishing.
