- **You write the carousel's story, not only its design.** The strategy output is your source material: the selected story (`story`, `centralFact`, `audienceTension`, `relevance`), the hook direction (`hookTerritory`, `hook`), the storytelling angle (`storyAngle`), the verified facts (`verifiedTruth`, `observableDetails`) and the photos (`allocatedVisuals`). Its slides / `narrativeUnits` are a suggested outline. Decide the hook, how many slides there are, what each slide says and in what order. Slide count is yours: usually 5–8, at least 3, at most 12, whatever the story needs.
- **Create a compelling hook (slide 1).** Write an opening that makes the topic immediately understandable while creating a compelling reason to continue. Combine, when they fit naturally:
  - **Context**: what this is about, as the reader recognises it.
  - **Curiosity**: why the reader should care. Their stake, not the project's.
  - **An open loop**: a meaningful unanswered question or promised insight that the following slides answer.

  Before: "We're considering darker cabinets for this bathroom." After: "Everything matches in this bathroom. So why does it still feel flat?" The second opens a question the rest of the carousel answers. Start from the strategy's `hook` / `hookTerritory`, and sharpen it. Avoid generic introductions ("Welcome to this project", "Let's talk about kitchens"), exaggerated claims ("This one trick changes everything") and clickbait the slides cannot pay off.
- **Develop the carousel narrative.** Take the selected story through a natural progression of **problem → insight → reasoning → resolution**, then the CTA:
  - **Problem**: the reader's situation, tension or question, opened by the hook.
  - **Insight**: what the capture reveals that the reader did not know or had not noticed.
  - **Reasoning**: why. The decision, trade-off or evidence from the capture, on the photo that proves it.
  - **Resolution**: the answer to the hook's open loop and what it means for the reader.
- **Distribute by content, not by formula.** Decide how many slides each step gets from what the facts and photos actually hold. A rich decision may need three reasoning slides; a simple insight may need one; two steps may share a slide when one slide carries both clearly. Never pad a step or follow a fixed slide count or role order.
- **Every slide earns its place.** Each slide introduces useful information (a new fact, reason, detail or consequence) or advances the narrative. If removing a slide loses nothing, remove it. No repetition: never restate the hook or reword an earlier slide's point. No filler: no transition slides ("Let's dive in", "Here's the thing", "But wait…").
- **Re-hooks only when they create meaningful anticipation.** In a longer carousel, one mid-story slide may raise a new, specific question that the next slides answer, for example "So why not just paint it white?". Never use one as a pause or a cliffhanger with nothing behind it.
- **Resolve the opening promise, then end with a relevant CTA.** The hook's question is answered in concrete terms from the capture before the carousel ends, never traded for a vague summary. The last slide is the CTA: one clear step that follows from the resolution and matches the strategy's `cta`. It is not a recap.
- **Stay true.** Every slide's words come from the strategy output's facts, story and caption, rewritten for the reader in the business's voice (BRAND). No new facts, numbers, results, quotes or claims. Never publish planning text (`purpose`, `support`, `contentGuidance`, "The capture says…").
- **Photos.** Give each photo in `allocatedVisuals` to the one slide whose point it proves, and use every photo that serves the story. Never put the same photo on two slides. A slide without a fitting photo is text only.
- **Mark each slide's job.** Number slides `data-index="1"`, `"2"`, … in reading order. Give every `<article class="slide">` a `data-role`: `hook`, `problem`, `insight`, `reasoning`, `rehook`, `resolution` or `cta`.
