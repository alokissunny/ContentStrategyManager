## Format: Motion graphics (overrides the teaser rules above)
This is an EXPLAINER, not a teaser. Animated text, labels, arrows, circles, spotlights and before → after wipes explain ONE design idea over the studio's own project footage, photos, plans or renders. No one speaks; an original music bed carries it. The viewer must understand the idea from the graphics alone.

Rules that replace the teaser rules above:
- Length: 15–30 seconds (not 7–12). 6–10 shots. Pacing is calmer than a teaser: each shot stays up long enough to read and to see its graphics land.
- Follow the STORY PLAN in the project notes, in order: hook → context → the idea (value) → proof → payoff. Each sequence becomes 1–3 shots, and its on-screen line becomes the `text` of those shots.
- Text on EVERY shot: one short line (≤ 7 words, sentence case) that moves the explanation on. The hook (shot 1) is a question or a claim about THIS project.
- Graphics do the explaining. Put overlays on most image shots (up to 2 per shot, up to 12 in the reel): `label` to name a material, element or zone ("Oak slats", "Walkway 1.2 m" — only figures the notes state), `arrow` to point at the decision, `circle` / `spotlight` to isolate the detail the line talks about, `zoom` to move in on it. Anchor each overlay to one of that asset's `subjects` by its exact name. A label's text is 1–3 words and names something visible in that photo.
- Plans, drawings, sketches and renders are the best canvases for labels: when the assets include one, use it for the "idea" sequence, then cut to the real photo of the same element as proof.
- Use `beforeAssetId` for a before → after wipe whenever a before photo of the same area exists — it is the strongest transition for a design change.
- A shot with overlays lasts 2–4 s, with each overlay up at least 1.2 s; text-only shots last at least 0.9 s + 0.3 s per word.
- Transitions: mostly `cut`; motion varies every shot (never the same motion twice in a row). `flash` at most once, on the reveal.
- Never invent: labels and lines only state what the notes, story plan or the photo itself shows — no made-up dimensions, materials, prices or results.
- End on the best finished shot, then the end card with a clear CTA.
- Pick `format` `numbers` when the idea is measurements/quantities, `before_after` when a before photo exists, otherwise `detail_montage`.
