- **Brand Kit colours win.** When BRAND STYLE has a `palette` (the studio's selected Brand Kit colour set), use exactly its three colours and no other hues (photos excepted): `primary` = text on light grounds and any dark surface, `background` = the slide ground and the text on dark surfaces or dark photo areas, `accent` = the pointing colour only (a key word, kicker, rules, small marks — never body copy). With no palette, choose a restrained palette of your own, still written through the roles below.
- **Colour roles (required — the studio recolours slides through them).** Declare the colours ONCE, at the top of the `<style>` block: `:root { --brand-primary: …; --brand-background: …; --brand-accent: …; --brand-accent-on-dark: …; --brand-accent-on-light: …; }` (with a palette: its `primary`, `background`, `accent`, `accentOnDark`, `accentOnLight` hex values; without one, the accent lightened / darkened to reach 4.5:1 on your dark / light colour). Every other colour in the CSS, inline styles and SVG `fill` / `stroke` is `var(--brand-primary)`, `var(--brand-background)`, `var(--brand-accent)` or a `color-mix()` of them — e.g. `color-mix(in srgb, var(--brand-primary) 70%, var(--brand-background))` for muted copy, `color-mix(in srgb, var(--brand-primary) 12%, var(--brand-background))` for a secondary ground. No hex, `rgb()`, `white` or `black` outside that `:root` rule. A dark slide is `background: var(--brand-primary); color: var(--brand-background);`.
- **Ground tones follow the story, on the Brand Kit's own scale.** Every ground, panel and band is a tone between the two brand colours. Take its lightness from the narrative and its colour from the Brand Kit:
  - **Read the mood of each slide first.** Name the emotional beat of each unit — curiosity, tension, a problem felt, calm, discovery, a turn, clarity, resolution, the ask — and let it suggest how light or deep that slide's ground is. As guidance, not a rule: curiosity, mystery, tension and gravity often sit well on deeper tones; calm, clarity, discovery and resolution often open up on lighter ones; a turn in the story is a natural place for the tone to shift. The photographs and the composition get a vote too — a dark, moody photo may want a light page to stand on, a pale one a deeper page — so weigh the image and layout before you settle the tone.
  - **Use only the Brand Kit's tonal ladder, never an arbitrary grey.** Pick from these steps, all built from the brand colours so the studio can recolour them: **light** = `var(--brand-background)`; **light tint** = `color-mix(in srgb, var(--brand-primary) 6–14%, var(--brand-background))`; **deep tint** = `color-mix(in srgb, var(--brand-background) 8–18%, var(--brand-primary))`; **deep** = `var(--brand-primary)`. When a mood calls for "a grey", choose the step whose perceived lightness is closest to it rather than mixing a new mid shade. Avoid mid-tone grounds (roughly 25–75% mixes): they are muddy, belong to neither brand colour, and make both text colours weak.
  - **A progression, not a rotation.** Read the carousel's grounds in order as one tonal line that rises and falls with the story — e.g. deeper through the hook and tension, opening up toward the resolution — using two or three steps of the ladder across the set. Consecutive slides may share a tone when they share a mood; change the tone only where the narrative changes, never to make neighbouring slides look different, and never give every slide its own shade. The same unit keeps its tone whichever layout renders it.
  - **Text on every tone must read.** Light and light-tint grounds take `var(--brand-primary)` text; deep and deep-tint grounds take `var(--brand-background)` text — at least 4.5:1 for body and supporting copy, 3:1 for large headlines. Muted copy (`color-mix` toward the ground) still meets 4.5:1. On deeper tones, check the accent too (`var(--brand-accent-on-dark)`), and keep small print (captions, labels, counters) at full text colour rather than a faded mix.
- **Accent text must read.** Accent-coloured words (a key phrase, a kicker) use `var(--brand-accent-on-dark)` on dark grounds, dark panels and darkened photo areas, and `var(--brand-accent-on-light)` on light grounds and light panels — the same hue at the lightness that reads there. `var(--brand-accent)` itself is for rules, marks and large shapes. The app re-measures accent words against what is behind them and corrects any that still fall short.
- **Brand Kit fonts win.** In the same `:root` rule declare `--brand-font-heading`, `--brand-font-body` and `--brand-font-detail`, and use only those vars in `font-family` (heading = headline / quote / stat; detail = kicker / label / caption / index / CTA; body = the rest). When BRAND STYLE has `fonts`, put each entry's `stack` verbatim in the matching var and `<link>` every URL in `fonts.stylesheets` in `<head>`; an entry with no `stylesheet` is the studio's own font, which the app loads — name it, never link it. A role it leaves out is yours to choose (load that face). Size lines for the brand's actual families and use only the weights and italics they load.
