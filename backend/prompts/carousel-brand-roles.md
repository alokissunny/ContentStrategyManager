- **Brand Kit colours win.** When BRAND STYLE has a `palette` (the studio's selected Brand Kit colour set), use exactly its three colours and no other hues (photos excepted): `primary` = text on light grounds and any dark surface, `background` = the slide ground and the text on dark surfaces or dark photo areas, `accent` = the pointing colour only (a key word, kicker, rules, small marks — never body copy). With no palette, choose a restrained palette of your own, still written through the roles below.
- **Colour roles (required — the studio recolours slides through them).** Declare the colours ONCE, at the top of the `<style>` block: `:root { --brand-primary: …; --brand-background: …; --brand-accent: …; --brand-accent-on-dark: …; --brand-accent-on-light: …; }` (with a palette: its `primary`, `background`, `accent`, `accentOnDark`, `accentOnLight` hex values; without one, the accent lightened / darkened to reach 4.5:1 on your dark / light colour). Every other colour in the CSS, inline styles and SVG `fill` / `stroke` is exactly `var(--brand-primary)`, `var(--brand-background)`, `var(--brand-accent)`, `var(--brand-accent-on-dark)` or `var(--brand-accent-on-light)` — never a `color-mix()` or opacity shade of them that would show as a new colour (no tinted grounds, no muted-grey copy, no faded rules). The one exception is a contrast wash behind text on a photo, which may fade a brand colour to `transparent`. No hex, `rgb()`, `white` or `black` outside that `:root` rule. A dark slide is `background: var(--brand-primary); color: var(--brand-background);`.
- **Grounds follow the story, in the Brand Kit's own colours — never a new shade.** Every ground, panel and band is one of the Brand Kit colours exactly as given. Never lighten, darken, tint or mix a Brand Kit colour to make a ground: it adds a colour the brand does not have and changes its look and feel. The only colour that may move in lightness is text (and accent marks) that would not read on its ground — that is what `--brand-accent-on-dark` / `--brand-accent-on-light` are for.
  - **Use lightness to choose, not to make.** Read the mood of each unit first — curiosity, tension, a problem felt, calm, discovery, a turn, clarity, resolution, the ask — and decide how light or deep that slide's ground should be on a greyscale from white to black. As guidance, not a rule: mystery, tension and gravity often want a deeper tone; calm, clarity, discovery and resolution often open up on a lighter one; a turn is a natural place to shift. The photographs and composition get a vote too. Then use the Brand Kit colour whose perceived lightness is closest to that target: usually `var(--brand-background)` for light and `var(--brand-primary)` for deep; `var(--brand-accent)` only for a single moment the story earns, and only when its text still reads. If the target sits between two colours, take the nearer one — never mix a shade in between.
  - **A progression, not a rotation.** Read the carousel's grounds in order as one line that rises and falls with the story. Consecutive slides share a ground when they share a mood; change it only where the narrative changes, never to make neighbouring slides look different. The same unit keeps its ground whichever layout renders it.
  - **Text on every ground must read.** Light grounds take `var(--brand-primary)` text; deep grounds take `var(--brand-background)` text — at least 4.5:1 for body and supporting copy, 3:1 for large headlines. Secondary copy (captions, labels, counters, supporting lines) uses the same full text colour, set apart by size and weight, not by a faded mix. If even the full text colour falls short on a ground, change the ground to another Brand Kit colour rather than inventing one.
- **Accent text must read.** Accent-coloured words (a key phrase, a kicker) use `var(--brand-accent-on-dark)` on dark grounds, dark panels and darkened photo areas, and `var(--brand-accent-on-light)` on light grounds and light panels — the same hue at the lightness that reads there. `var(--brand-accent)` itself is for rules, marks and large shapes. The app re-measures accent words against what is behind them and corrects any that still fall short.
- **Brand Kit fonts win.** In the same `:root` rule declare `--brand-font-heading`, `--brand-font-body` and `--brand-font-detail`, and use only those vars in `font-family` (heading = headline / quote / stat; detail = kicker / label / caption / index / CTA; body = the rest). When BRAND STYLE has `fonts`, put each entry's `stack` verbatim in the matching var and `<link>` every URL in `fonts.stylesheets` in `<head>`; an entry with no `stylesheet` is the studio's own font, which the app loads — name it, never link it. A role it leaves out is yours to choose (load that face). Size lines for the brand's actual families and use only the weights and italics they load.
